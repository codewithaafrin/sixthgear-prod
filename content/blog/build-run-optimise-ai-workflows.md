**An AI workflow delivers lasting value only when it is built, run and continuously optimised against a business KPI.** Launching it is the start, not the finish. Inputs change, models are updated, edge cases appear and business rules evolve, so a workflow that is not actively managed drifts and its results decline.

## What does "build" involve?

Designing the workflow around a measurable outcome: mapping the process, choosing models and tools, integrating with your systems, and deciding where people review AI output. A good build starts from the KPI, not from the technology.

## What does "run" involve?

Operating the workflow in production like any critical system:

- Monitoring volume, accuracy, latency and cost.
- Handling exceptions and escalations to people.
- Keeping integrations, credentials and data pipelines healthy.
- Providing coverage around the clock where the business needs it, including 24/7 operations.

## What does "optimise" involve?

Using results to improve the workflow every week or month:

- Reviewing errors and human corrections, then tuning prompts, rules and routing.
- Testing newer or cheaper models as they become available.
- Expanding automation to more of the process as confidence grows.
- Reporting progress against the agreed KPI, such as hours saved, turnaround or share of queries resolved.

## Why does this matter?

Most of the value of AI automation compounds after launch. A support agent that resolves a growing share of routine queries, or a data workflow that steadily cuts manual entry, only gets there through continuous tuning. Sixth Gear's case studies show the kind of results this produces: 150× faster first reply in customer service and 65% less manual data entry in field operations.

## Key takeaways

- Build around a KPI, run it like production software, and optimise continuously.
- Expect drift; plan who monitors and tunes the workflow.
- Report results in business terms, not AI activity.
