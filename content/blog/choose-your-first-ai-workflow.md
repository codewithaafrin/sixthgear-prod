**The best first workflow to automate with AI is one that is high-volume, repetitive, measurable and low-risk if the AI gets something wrong.** It should have a clear owner, data you can already access, and a single KPI everyone agrees on, such as hours spent, turnaround time or share of requests resolved without a person.

## What makes a good first AI workflow?

Score each candidate process from 1 to 5 on these six criteria and start with the highest total:

| Criterion | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Volume | Happens dozens or hundreds of times a week |
| Repetition | Most cases follow a similar pattern |
| Measurability | You can count time, cost, speed or errors today |
| Data access | Inputs and systems are reachable via APIs or exports |
| Risk | Mistakes are visible and reversible; a human can review |
| Ownership | One person is accountable and wants it fixed |

## Where do teams usually find the best candidates?

Look for bottlenecks: the steps where work waits. Common first workflows include:

- **Support triage and investigation**: classifying tickets, gathering context, reproducing issues.
- **Document and data entry**: extracting fields from forms, reports or emails into your systems.
- **Report drafting**: turning raw data and notes into a first-draft report for review.
- **Routine customer queries**: answering the repetitive questions that make up most of the volume.

## What should you avoid as a first project?

Avoid processes that are rare, highly political, dependent on data you cannot access, or where one wrong answer causes serious harm. Those can come later, once you have proven value and built trust.

## How do you turn the choice into a pilot?

1. **Map the workflow**: write down every step, hand-off and system involved.
2. **Assess readiness and risks**: data quality, integrations, compliance and who reviews AI output.
3. **Build the case**: estimate the cost to build and run against the expected KPI improvement.
4. **Plan the pilot**: a time-boxed rollout on real work, with a baseline measured before launch.

## Key takeaways

- Start where volume is high, patterns repeat and the KPI is easy to measure.
- Choose a process where mistakes are cheap and reviewable.
- Measure the baseline before you automate, so you can prove the result.
