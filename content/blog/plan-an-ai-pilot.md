**A good AI pilot takes four steps: map the workflow, assess readiness and risks, build a case estimating costs and outcomes, then run a time-boxed pilot on real work against a baseline you measured beforehand.** The goal is not a demo. It is evidence that one real process improves on one agreed KPI.

## Step 1: Identify bottlenecks and map the workflow

Write down every step of the process, who does it, which systems are touched, and where work waits. The waiting points (hand-offs, manual data entry, repeated investigation) are your bottlenecks and usually the best place to apply AI first.

## Step 2: Assess readiness and risks

Answer these before building:

- **Data**: can the workflow access the inputs it needs, and are they reliable?
- **Systems**: are there APIs or exports for the tools involved?
- **Risk**: what happens if the AI is wrong, and who reviews its output?
- **Compliance**: are there privacy, security or regulatory constraints?
- **People**: who owns the process and will champion the change?

## Step 3: Build a case estimating costs and outcomes

Estimate the build cost, running costs (models, infrastructure, human review) and the expected improvement on your KPI. Agree on what success looks like, for example "cut turnaround time in half" or "save 10 hours per person per week."

## Step 4: Plan the pilot

- **Scope**: one process, one team, real work.
- **Duration**: time-boxed, typically a few weeks, long enough to see stable results.
- **Baseline**: measure the KPI before launch.
- **Checkpoints**: weekly reviews of results, errors and user feedback.
- **Exit criteria**: decide in advance what result means "scale it," "tune it" or "stop."

## What happens after a successful pilot?

Scale gradually to more volume or similar processes, and keep running and optimising the workflow. AI workflows are not "set and forget". Inputs, models and business rules change, and performance should be managed against the KPI continuously.

## Key takeaways

- Map → assess → build the case → pilot. Do it in that order.
- Measure the baseline first and define exit criteria up front.
- Plan who runs and optimises the workflow after the pilot.
