01 / The pain points

Three weeks to lose the only engineer who knew support.

Every technical ticket ran through one person, and he was leaving.

  1. 01

    One person held all the technical knowledge.

    A single engineer handled every ticket that needed the code or the account data. He was leaving in three weeks, with no handover plan.

  2. 02

    Customers waited too long.

    Median first reply was 9 hours on weekdays and 26 hours over weekends, in a product where a broken site is urgent.

  3. 03

    Technical tickets stalled.

    One in four of ~60 monthly conversations needed a developer, and those took 2.3 days on average to resolve.

  4. 04

    The founder was doing support.

    He spent 6.5 hours a week answering tickets instead of building the product.

  5. 05

    Answers drifted from the product.

    The help docs lagged behind the code, and 14% of replies had to be corrected in a follow-up message.

  6. 06

    Churn was spotted after the fact.

    Cancellations surfaced in the billing dashboard days later, with no view of why or who was next.

  7. 07

    Hiring wasn’t a real option.

    A replacement would cost over $70,000 a year and take two to three months to ramp up on the codebase.

02 / What we built

One agent that answers, investigates and fixes.

Scoped to the support ticket, while the client’s team keeps the final say.

01

Drafts inside the help desk

Every new message gets a ready-to-send reply plus a private note showing what was checked and where.

02

Account-aware answers

Read-only access to the customer’s account, billing, product analytics, logs and live DNS, so replies reflect their real plan, sites and charges.

03

Answers checked against the code

When the knowledge base and production code disagree, the agent holds the reply and flags it.

04

Debugging to the line of code

The agent rebuilds the customer’s site in a sealed sandbox, reproduces the fault and traces its cause.

05

Fixes as draft pull requests

A PR opens only when the failing reproduction passes after the change. Merging stays with the team.

06

Weekly customer pulse

A Monday summary of new signups, cancellations, likely churn reasons and at-risk accounts.

It runs on a hardened private server with read-only credentials, and every lookup is limited to the one customer in the conversation.
